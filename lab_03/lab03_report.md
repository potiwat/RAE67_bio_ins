# Lab 03 Report — SO(2) CPG and alternating tripod gait

## 1. Objective
This Lab studies the SO(2) CPG update rule, the alternating tripod decoder, and the bounded mapping from a slow modulation input `m(t)` to `phi` and stride. The goal is to verify how phase increment and recurrent gain change gait timing and foot clearance, while keeping a valid three-leg support pattern.

## 2. Implementation status
The required functions were completed in `student_cpg.py`:
- `so2_step` uses the simultaneous SO(2) update
- `decode_tripod` outputs `x_rel`, `lift`, and `contact` for all six legs
- `bounded_modulation` clamps `phi` and stride to their bounds

## 3. Gait comparison result
The batch experiment used the default reference conditions:
- baseline: `alpha=1.10`, `phi=0.20 rad`
- fast: `alpha=1.10`, `phi=0.40 rad`
- high_gain: `alpha=1.30`, `phi=0.20 rad`

Summary table:

| Condition | Speed (m/s) | Frequency (Hz) | Duty factor | Min support | Clearance (mm) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| baseline | 0.077756 | 0.625000 | 0.494176 | 3 | 17.095 |
| fast | 0.155511 | 1.267123 | 0.500832 | 3 | 17.113 |
| high_gain | 0.094457 | 0.544218 | 0.494176 | 3 | 24.637 |

### Interpretation
- Increasing `phi` from 0.20 to 0.40 rad approximately doubled both the measured speed and oscillation frequency, confirming that faster phase rotation shortens the stepping cycle.
- Increasing `alpha` from 1.10 to 1.30 increased amplitude and clearance substantially, but the frequency decreased slightly because the system saturated more strongly under the `tanh` nonlinearity.
- The minimum support remained at 3 legs in all conditions because the alternating tripod decoder enforces a 3-leg stance with opposite-phase tripod groups.

## 6. Scope and limitations
This model validates the logic of timing, phase, foot placement, and metric extraction, but it is not a full rigid-body or contact-force simulation. It does not demonstrate actuator torque, friction, energy efficiency, or dynamic stability of a real robot.
